### 2. Answers to the questions

1. **Sent request ending as failed, or sent again by the product?** In the secret protocol, only a post-send 503 classed as definite (R2-3). Sent again: only the provisioner's queue retry and the archive tick (R2-2). A secret change is never sent twice.
2. **Can another worker take over an effect?** Not for secrets or credential issuance: `claimDispatch` and `finishOperation` are bound to the executor's boot id and the sweep only marks. Yes for the provisioner (60 s stale claim, pg-boss expiry) and the archive tick (R2-2), and a worker that lost its claim can overwrite the new owner's result (R2-8).
3. **Can a replay return another outcome, or be read by someone who may not?** Yes, through Unicode normalisation (R2-6); temporary refusals replay unchanged for 30 days (R2-5). Read by someone who may not: none. The key holds the principal, and the replay is looked up only after authorisation.
4. **Can a write land without the expected version and state tag?** None found. `versionedRow` requires both, and an import update without a precondition throws before admission.
5. **Is anything reported done before the backend confirmed it?** None found. Every write, delete and undelete is proven by read-back; provisioning activates only after its read-backs.
6. **Does reconciliation declare "no effect" without evidence?** None found. `rejected` needs executor exclusion and the backend at its starting version; delete and undelete are judged only with exclusion.
7. **Is "the action was not performed" true on every `audit_unavailable` path?** True for the requested action, but a review state can be written without its audit row while the caller is told nothing happened (R2-4).
8. **Can a read return part of a set?** None found. The backend's name list is compared with the index, every live value is read or nothing is returned, the generation and the slot are checked again, and the audit row is committed before release.

### Answers that change

- **Q1 (a request that may have been sent ends as failed, or is sent again):** yes, in narrow cases. R2-3 (a 503 after the marker is stored as rejected) and R2-10 (the archive answers 503 after revocations may have gone out). R2-9 and R2-11 make the proof of "no effect" weaker. The product never sends a secret change again by itself; R2-2 lists the exceptions by design.
- **Q2 (another worker takes over an effect):**
  - Secret changes: no. The sweep clears the boot id, and a late executor can neither dispatch nor finalize (`sweep.test.ts` "a swept executor cannot finalize", `records.test.ts`).
  - Provisioner: yes after 60 s, and `end()` does not check that the claim is still its own (R2-8).
  - Archive tick: yes, by design (D-41).
- **Q3 (a replay returns another outcome, or is read by someone who may not):** another outcome, yes:
  - for byte-different values that are NFC-equivalent (R2-6);
  - when a transient refusal was stored (R2-5).

  Read by someone who may not: no. `replay.test.ts` gives 404 after the grant is removed.

- **Q6 (reconciliation declares "no effect" without evidence):** only on the operator's unchecked statement that executors are excluded (R2-11), plus R2-9's loose 400. A write that landed is always proven by its envelope, without exclusion.
- Q4, Q5, Q7 and Q8 are unchanged:
  - **Q4 (a write lands without the expected version and state tag):** no; `exclusion.test.ts` has 20 executors and exactly one write.
  - **Q5 (something reported done before the backend confirmed it):** no.
  - **Q7 (`audit_unavailable` means the action was not performed):** true on every path I read, with one side effect: an environment or key review mark can land without its audit row (R2-4).
  - **Q8 (a read returns part of a set):** no. `complete.test.ts` (the Nth backend read fails: no entries) and `race.test.ts` (every read equals the set of its generation).
